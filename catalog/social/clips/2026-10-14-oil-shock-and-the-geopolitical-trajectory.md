- **Caption:** JT explains why he values suspending judgment in remote viewing: he wants to separate a viewer's impressions from the explanations their mind adds.
- **Hashtags:** #RemoteViewing #PeakMind
- **Review:** [Play this short](https://matts-macbook-pro.tailebcf61.ts.net:5480/media/kits/shorts-week-03/41%20-%20Suspend%20judgment.mp4)
- **Studio:** [Clips tab](https://matts-macbook-pro.tailebcf61.ts.net:5480/#ep-2026-10-14)

> And I know enough viewers to know that, generally speaking, you don't have a lot of polymaths in remote viewing. You don't have a lot of experts in a number of subjects. That's not to be critical. It's just not the profile that you generally find with experienced or successful remote viewers. The exact opposite is true. You have people who tend to suspend judgment on many subjects. And that is a strength when they become remote viewers because you want to minimize analytical overlay. You want to minimize the amount that your mind fills in when you're in a remote viewing session or the effort your mind puts in to explain things to you. You want to set that aside and just get the data itself.
