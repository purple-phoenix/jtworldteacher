### 2. Reading bias into the same article

- **Time:** `354.300` → `387.100` seconds on the edited Spotify kit audio. Continuous cut, 32.80 s.
- **Speaker:** JT
- **Slot:** 2026-11-28, 12:00 PM ET for dated selections. Awaiting Matt.
- **Why it works:** A compact social observation, cut before the dated Canada discussion.
- **Caption:** JT describes how people can read opposing political biases into the same article, and project their own assumptions onto another person's words.
- **Hashtags:** #MediaLiteracy #PeakMind
- **Review:** [Play this short](https://matts-macbook-pro.tailebcf61.ts.net:5480/media/kits/shorts-week-03/36%20-%20Reading%20bias%20into%20a%20post.mp4)
- **Studio:** [Clips tab](https://matts-macbook-pro.tailebcf61.ts.net:5480/#ep-2026-10-21)

> People often see things that you say and they'll read into it, right? And they'll project onto it and they'll sort of, I think there was a study once that said if they showed people the same article, people on the political right would say the article had a left-wing bias and people on the left would say the article had a right-wing bias. People just tend to, not everyone, but people tend to project those things onto other people's opinions. So it's important to have that disclaimer that this is just observation rather than reflecting a political ideology on my part.
