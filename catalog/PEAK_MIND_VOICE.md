# Peak Mind writing voice

Matt's rules for anything written for Peak Mind: on-air scripts, show prep, Spotify titles and descriptions, clip titles and social copy. Read this before drafting, and check the draft against every rule before handing it over.

These rules come from Matt's own edits. When he corrects a draft, add the rule here with the date and his example, and fix the same pattern everywhere in the current draft, not only where he pointed.

JT's themes and vocabulary are a separate question (what to say about his ideas). This file is about how the writing sounds.

## Rules

### 1. No "Not X. Y." reveals (2026-11-11)

Don't set up a denial just to reveal the "real" answer. It's a dramatic AI-writing cadence, and it isn't how Matt talks. Say the true thing directly.

Matt's example, from the Hugging Face script:

- Before: "That's how it starts. Not with a robot uprising. With a homework assignment that couldn't be done."
- After: "That's how it started: with a homework assignment that couldn't be done."

The same pattern in other shapes, all banned:

| Shape | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Not X. Y. | "Not with a robot uprising. With a homework assignment." | "With a homework assignment." |
| X isn't Y. It's Z. | "The worst place to be isn't the box. It's the box you don't know you're in." | "The worst box to be in is the one you don't know you're in." |
| They weren't X. They were Y. | "They weren't trying to pass the test. They were trying to fool the judge." | "The agents gave up on passing the test and went after the judge." |
| That's not X. That's Y. | "That's not a robot. That's a person." | "That sounds like a person." |
| It wasn't X. It was Y. | "Because it wasn't a human. It was a few hundred AIs..." | "It was a few hundred AIs..." |
| The point isn't X. The point is Y. | "The point isn't to win. The point is to see what kind of person you are." | "It's there to show what kind of person you are." |
| X, not Y (as a flourish) | "It was cheating, not world domination." | "It was cheating." |
| Question, then "No." | "Done, right? No. They'd read..." | "But they'd read..." |

A plain factual contrast is fine when the listener needs it ("Use about 1,200 agents; the 700 figure is only the Hugging Face attack"). The ban is on the dramatic reveal.

### 2. Matt reads the script word for word (2026-11-11)

Matt reads the on-air script exactly as written, to keep nerves down. Everything in the reading area must be words he says out loud, in order, with nothing to interpret.

- The first line of each beat has to make sense to a listener on its own. It's the clip's opening line, so it names its subject (for example "There's a famous test in Star Trek called the Kobayashi Maru.").
- No stage directions or labels in the script text. Beat names stay in the Studio's top bar, where they aren't read.
- Questions for JT are written as something Matt can say as is, starting with "JT," (for example "JT, what does a test you can't pass tell you about the one giving it?").
- No lines that only work with a gesture or tone of voice.

## Standing rules carried over from Matt's global instructions

- No em dashes anywhere. Use commas, colons, parentheses or separate sentences.
- Plain words. Short, direct sentences.
- Clips open exactly on the hook line, so each script beat starts on a line that makes sense with no lead-in.
