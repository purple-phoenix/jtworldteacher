## About this episode

**Morgan Farrell** of **Intuitive Underground** is back for part two, with JT's brother Matt again running the interview and asking the newcomer's questions. Asked for a project that excited him, Morgan goes back to week three of his training: a front-loaded, predictive exercise to remote view the main photo on the next morning's front page. He ran the session, walked to the corner store at 8 a.m., and found the picture he had sketched — Queen Elizabeth waving to a crowd from the deck of a ship. Pressed by JT for the elevator-pitch answer, he tells the story of late February 2016, when three blind viewers' sessions were each strong enough on their own to call the presidential election for Donald Trump (Morgan named him in session, which is rare — "sketch and describe, don't name"). The kicker came on election night, when the team member who had tasked the project watched the result come in on Morgan's couch and said, "I didn't think that was going to happen."

That opens the episode's running argument about **belief and proof**. JT's view: even well-known practitioners often don't fully believe in what they do; unpopular predictions draw hostility (Morgan estimates 80% or more of the response to the group's 2024 election call was negative) and correct ones rarely draw an apology; and because, by his figure, some 40% of people can't perceive this at all, you can never "prove" it to everyone. Matt asks the practical questions — what is the group's mission, can you make money with this, do you track a hit rate? Morgan's answers: Intuitive Underground began as a study group and exists to demonstrate the power of remote viewing so that people learn to do it themselves (Ingo Swann's *Secrets of Power*, teach a man to fish, "a world without secrets"); he calls his own viewing "utilitarian," and says the strength is the team. He knows someone who bet the 2016 call at five to one. And he is an operations man, not a laboratory man — he needs the team to make the right call, not a database — though he walks through session grading and the **Targ scale**. JT points to Chase McCarthy's socialrv.com for the numbers (about 51% across roughly 20,000 open-to-everyone sessions) and to Lyn Buchanan's per-viewer tracking, and argues at length that the right comparison isn't 100% but the alternatives: experts who are right about half the time.

Asked for the most important thing people should know about the work, Morgan gives the group's answer: the **nature of reality**. A teammate tasked the target "the most significant remote viewing target," and what came back was a storyboard — major earth changes, mass death, then a "harvest" of the newly dead and a forced reincarnation cycle — that led them, line of inquiry by line of inquiry, to a world that is "no real world at all": something like a simulation, but with a biological component, closer to a shared and regulated dream. JT ties it to Buddhism, Hinduism, the Gnostics, and the Manichaeans. The episode closes with Morgan's warning about any psychic who has never had an unhappy client, JT's goal of taking the podcast to Contact in the Desert in June 2027, and Morgan's free primer at remoteviewingtraining.com.

## Show notes

- **Morgan's first big hit** — a week-three training lesson in front-loaded, predictive work: remote view the primary front-page photo of a specific newspaper, then check the corner-store rack the next morning; a missed qualifier in the first targeting cue, corrected on the old rvcommunity.net forums; the second session matching a photo of Queen Elizabeth addressing a crowd from a ship
- **Practitioners who don't believe** — JT on well-known remote viewers and astrologers being shocked when their own predictions land; his aside on Dan Waites of World Astrology Report
- **Morgan's documentation** — recordings of the conversations before, during, and after sessions, so nothing rests on hearsay
- **The 2016 election project** — late February 2016, three viewers tasked blind plus a project manager; Morgan naming Donald Trump in session against the rule "sketch and describe, don't name"; a round-table debrief while still blind; results published in early March; the former team member on Morgan's couch at 3:30 a.m.: "I didn't think that was going to happen"
- **Hostility and apologies** — 2016 pushback was personal; the 2024 election prediction drew, by Morgan's estimate, 80% or more negative responses; people who come back to say "you were right" are a single-digit percentage
- **JT on why you can't prove it to everyone** — unpopular predictions read as politics or doom-mongering; being ghosted after predictions come true; his figure that 40% of people lack extrasensory perception and can't believe it's real; belief as a virtue signal and a form of conformity
- **The mission** — Intuitive Underground's start as a study group of students who trained around the same time; going operational; "demonstrating the power of remote viewing" on targets interesting or important to the public; predictive intelligence as the through line; a published track record as the foundation
- **Individual empowerment** — Ingo Swann's two-volume *Secrets of Power*; give a man a fish or teach a man to fish; remote viewing as "a world without secrets," good for many and a threat to others
- **Superstars vs. teams** — JT on the field's pyramid of star viewers; Morgan's guitar analogy (you may never be a rock star, but nobody regrets learning); his self-description as a "utilitarian" viewer with long-term reliability; a team of that level leaving "not a whole lot we can't do"
- **The hardest report Morgan has written** — translating session work on the medical intervention introduced in late 2020 into proper medical language, seven to ten days of full-time work; JT's summary of what the report anticipated (a dialysis-style treatment, colloidal metals), flagged with "I'm not a doctor"; the report is free at intuitiveunderground.com (event, not endorsement)
- **Can you make money with it?** — JT lists groups doing associative remote viewing and crypto forecasting and calls most crypto a pyramid scheme; Morgan never thought to bet the 2016 call but knows someone who did, at five to one, and has seen the slip; JT's Holyfield–Tyson story; JT's unconfirmed-rumor aside about Epstein-adjacent lottery wins
- **Hit rates** — perception-by-perception grading (correct, probably correct, can't feedback, incorrect); laboratory vs. operational camps; why a 100%-correct but vague session is useless (the watermelon example); Russell Targ's one-to-seven **Targ scale**; how grading can discourage viewers from taking risks
- **Who tracks the numbers** — Chase McCarthy's socialrv.com putting sessions on the blockchain (about 51% across roughly 20,000 sessions open to everyone, higher for his "super viewers"); the 65–85% range quoted for professionals; Lyn Buchanan's spreadsheets matching viewers to subject types; the "heroic individual" model vs. TRV's team aggregate and the wisdom of the crowd
- **Bell curve of effort** — the least and the most naturally psychic students both work hardest; strong natural psychics struggle to conform to a rigid structure, then improve markedly once they do
- **The most significant remote viewing target** — a storyboard of major earth changes, mass die-off, a "harvest" of the recently dead, the "death trap" of forced reincarnation; the death process; reality as a simulation with a biological component — "a shared and regulated dream state," with a Neuralink-like system as the modern analogy; Morgan and Dan first discussed it publicly on JT's "Bigfoot" Space
- **JT's context** — the same idea in Buddhism, Hinduism, Greek antiquity, the Gnostics, and the Manichaeans; once you admit it, everything is also a symbol
- **JT's long argument for acting on imperfect data** — surgery outcomes aren't 100% either; people crowd toward the middle of the group for safety; status as the real currency; why gurus never really teach you to compete with them; the wisdom of crowds vs. experts who are right about 51% of the time; "I take 65 to 85% any day"
- **Who actually uses this** — the JP Morgan line about billionaires and astrologers; companies timing hotel openings by astrology; dozens to hundreds of operational remote viewing groups working under NDA; JT's claims that Lue Elizondo and Richard Doty are trained remote viewers, and that Prudence Calabrese and John Vivanco worked with the FBI after 9/11
- **Ethics** — JT on clients with limited incomes and when he would step back; Matt on why a published track record matters; remote viewers as artsy, mostly non-materialistic types
- **Morgan's safeguards** — noise filters at each step from project conception to report; never publishing a result the team isn't satisfied with; caveating contract work; the client who refused to act on an unwelcome family insight; beware anyone who claims they've never had a displeased client — that's "no better than a cold read"
- **Housekeeping** — JT is saving to hire remote viewers, through Morgan, for his own medical questions; the goal of bringing the podcast to **Contact in the Desert**, June 3–7, 2027; Morgan's free ~50-page primer at remoteviewingtraining.com
