## About this episode

JT bills this one as "character development": no guest, just the two brothers, starting with Matt's two weeks near Playa del Carmen and the three Maya sites he visited. **Chichén Itzá** has the vendors and the crowds, but also the clap that echoes back as the call of the quetzal and the equinox serpent of light on the steps. **Cobá** was his favorite — a bike ride through the jungle to Nohoch Mul, one of the last pyramids you can still climb. **Tulum** is the walled port on the water, still trading when the Spanish arrived. The detail that sticks is what a guide told him: the pyramids are "sacred man-made mountains," built only as tall as they needed to be to clear the canopy and see the stars, and they sit over **cenotes** — the limestone sinkholes that were the Maya connection to the underworld, where tree roots twist together like ropes thick enough to hug and push down through fifty feet of rock to reach the water.

That image gave JT an epiphany: the pyramids are artificial monuments to the **world tree**. He brings in the lore around Devils Tower — which he says the Native people of the area call the grandmother tree and regard as the living stump of a giant tree (he is careful to say he isn't claiming it's true, only that he wouldn't bet on the scientists over the elders). Matt then recounts a recent **Rick and Morty** episode in which a sentient tree traps souls in a respawning evolutionary simulation and feeds on their killing each other; the only way out is to stop. JT: "they inserted the story of the archons into the metaphor of the tree." From there he lays out his answer to the injustice of the simulation — natural selection as "externalizing one's suffering," industrial animal farming as the ultimate example, a prisoner's dilemma that every spiritual movement began by recognizing — notes that the simulation is an emerging archetype in fiction, and casts the tree in the role of the **demiurge**, which he identifies, by way of the book *The Cosmic Serpent*, the doctrine of signatures, and biophotons, with DNA itself.

The last hour is astrology and current events. JT concedes that Vedic astrology's **nakshatras** and lunar nodes capture something Western astrology doesn't: the invisible, internal organization of human affairs by the collective unconscious. Eclipses are where that shows — Babylonian omen-watching, substitute kings, and his rule of thumb that a prominent man leaves the room at every solar eclipse. The **August 12 south-node eclipse at 20° Leo**, with the south node hovering over Regulus, is in his reading a fated changing of the guard, to be activated as Jupiter and Mars cross the eclipse point: around October 3, on November 3 — US election day — and finally between June 7 and July 12, 2027. He declines to predict which party. He reads the brothers' own podcast as part of the same reshuffle, and closes with a long warning — AI, an escalating war, and a fuel crisis, as he sees them — that this may be the last window of physical and social mobility, so do the thing you've been dreaming of before next July.

## Show notes

- **"Character development"** — a more personal episode after two weeks with Morgan Farrell; Matt introduces himself and his first trip to Mexico: a resort near Playa del Carmen found with the help of AI, mostly Mexican vacationers, "on vacation rather than traveling"
- **"Maya," not "Mayan"** — a guide's correction (Mayan is the language); the pros and cons of resort excursions
- **Chichén Itzá** — one of the new seven wonders; a path lined with vendors; the clap on the north side that echoes back as the call of the quetzal, the bird of Quetzalcoatl; the equinox light-and-shadow serpent on the steps; no more climbing
- **Cenotes** — sinkholes of water filtered through limestone, some as deep as the pyramids are tall; sacred sites with offerings and bones on the bottom; the contention over swimming in them; the guide's claim that the Maya prized silver over gold
- **Cobá** — Matt's favorite and the least touristy; bikes from a distant parking lot past observatories; Nohoch Mul, the tallest pyramid in that part of the Yucatán and one of the last you can climb, now with added stairs
- **Man-made mountains** — flat terrain, so pyramids built just high enough to clear the canopy and watch the stars; cenotes beneath the pyramids, including one under Chichén Itzá reachable only by scuba with special permission
- **Tulum** — a walled trading port on the water, the newest of the three and active when the Spanish arrived; solstice and equinox alignments; handprints with six or seven fingers, which the guide attributed to royal inbreeding
- **Star brothers and blue orbs** — New Age lore about why climbing was stopped, which JT labels probable urban myth and "conspiratainment"
- **The pyramids as world trees** — Matt's message about roots reaching down into the cenote while the pyramid rises above the canopy; Mount Kailash and the Great Pyramid as parallels; JT's epiphany that the pyramids are artificial monuments to the world tree
- **Devils Tower as the grandmother tree** — as JT tells it: bark-like columns, a flat top like a cut stump, ring-like patterns, elders' accounts of a still-living root system feeding the trees for hundreds of miles; the age of giant mushrooms before trees; pareidolia as the skeptical answer; "I wouldn't necessarily bet on the scientists"; the world tree as a universal archetype, down to *Avatar*
- **The Rick and Morty episode** — Matt's recap: a sentient tree pulls Rick and Morty's essence into an underground simulation where everyone starts as an amoeba, dies, respawns, and evolves; Rick goes to war, Morty goes peaceful and becomes a plant; the reveal that the tree feeds on the energy of their killing each other; the escape is to stop killing
- **JT's reading** — the archon story inserted into the world-tree metaphor; why "love and light" spirituality struggles with the simulation idea; the Intuitive Underground material and the Apocryphon of John
- **The injustice problem** — occult currents that answer the prison by pleasing the guards ("the secret of life is to do what you want"), with JT's passing reference to networks like Epstein's; his alternative: set aside moralizing and stop cooperating
- **Externalized suffering** — natural selection as delaying your own suffering at others' expense; agriculture as the escape from seasonal starvation; the "vast, globalized, industrialized network of animal torture" as the ultimate metaphor; the prisoner's dilemma; the same recognition at the root of early Christianity, the Therapeutae and Essenes, Zoroastrianism, Buddhism, Sufism, and Hinduism
- **The simulation as an emerging archetype** — like zombies before the pandemic or the danse macabre around the Black Death; the newer the medium, the less diluted the signal; tech bros appropriating simulationism
- **Trees think** — JT, a self-described rough working-class guy from Boston with Buddhist leanings, on what biologists have found: trees communicating and sharing sugar through their root networks, feeding the stumps of cut trees, crown shyness
- **The tree as demiurge** — the demiurge as the internal ruler of the reality overlay; the book JT calls *The Cosmic Serpent*: ayahuasca visions of entwined snakes and the lion-headed serpent read as DNA; the **doctrine of signatures**; **biophotons** and a light-based network linking all DNA; "DNA is the cosmic serpent. It's the demiurge."
- **A consulting aside** — JT advised against a Mexico trip earlier in the year when fighting seemed likely to spread; fate meets you wherever you are, but an astrologer asked about a dangerous place has to say no
- **Finding the show's voice** — the brothers' age gap and first joint project; JT's non-materialism and his catastrophic injury as a "cautionary tale"; Matt's security-mindedness as a useful corrective; why remote viewers need a way to fund their work, and JT's discomfort with crypto forecasting ("largely it's gambling")
- **Vedic vs. Western astrology** — JT's argument that much of Vedic astrology arrived with the Greeks; what he now grants it: the **nakshatras** (lunar mansions) and the nodes, Rahu and Ketu, as a study of unconscious influence; Western astrology's focus on external, fated events
- **Intrusions from the collective unconscious** — art, fiction, myth, and synchronicity as the purposeful internal organization of the world, against an external organization that keeps growing more onerous
- **Eclipses and kings** — Babylonian astrology compiled over roughly 500 years of omen-watching; solar eclipses and the departure of prominent men; substitute rulers installed to be "eclipsed out," a practice JT says continues today
- **The eclipse in Leo** — the August 12, 2026 south-node eclipse at 20° Leo; the 19-year echo (1999 and 2018 at 19° Leo as north-node eclipses; 2007 and 2026 as south-node ones), so August 2007 themes may rhyme now; the south node over the royal star **Regulus** at about 29° Leo; the Chris Bledsoe prediction, to be untangled in a future episode
- **The activations** — Jupiter crossing the eclipse point and returning twice more, the last pass on June 7, 2027; Mars crossing it on November 3, US election day, then retrograding back to about 21°; two more Jupiter–Saturn trines, the last next July; JT's timeframe: around October 3, around November 3, then resolution between June 7 and July 12
- **What he will and won't predict** — a large swath of leadership removed by acts of fate, emerging figures "conceived" as future rulers, possibly a martial event; no call on which party; Leo also rules children, leisure, and influencers, for whom he expects a backlash like the one against the petty nobility in the 1780s and '90s
- **Your own chart** — planets or points within about five degrees in the fixed signs; spotlight and leadership changes on a small scale, including the brothers' podcast coming together "suddenly, unexpectedly" around the eclipse
- **The closing window** — JT's view that this may be the last period of this level of physical and social mobility: AI job losses arriving sooner than forecast, the end of the working-class-to-middle-class dream
- **One war** — JT's account of conflicts in Africa, the Middle East, and Ukraine as a single escalating war, plus the fuel situation as he describes it: refinery attacks, Europe's winter gas, refineries sited near customers rather than producers, diesel export bans, stockpiles and pipeline pressure, and a US government he calls "incredibly competent" at holding it together so far (his reading of events, not reporting)
- **Practical advice** — a couple of weeks of food, extra prescriptions, no need to "go prepper"; he says the draft age is slated to rise and tells listeners to check for themselves; pursue goals and travel before next July, a period of preparation through July 11
- **Housekeeping** — remote-viewing-based material on these themes promised soon; a podcast planned for the day after the election; aiming for shorter episodes and maybe a separate discussion Space; **Contact in the Desert** as the goal, lining up with Jupiter's final pass over the eclipse point
