## About this episode

This Space is JT's takedown of Dan Waites' World Astrology Report and his "Astro RV" sessions, with Josh Maitland catching strays as collateral. JT's beef is that Dan and Josh crossed the streams, grabbed the Astro RV concept that Morgan Farrell and Intuitive Underground have been developing for over a decade as part of their Canon project, and turned a serious predictive protocol into influencer entertainment. Dan got 400,000 views on his April 19th prediction video — building explosions, ships, capsules, UFOs in space — and JT argues those weren't real RV hits; they were the viewers picking up Dan's own preoccupations because Dan doesn't have the experience to target a session properly.

The technical case: Dan doesn't grasp the difference between mundane and natal astrology, picked a mundane chart (the nuclear age chart) for a session that needed natal-style targeting, and chose April 19th when JT had publicly called April 11-12 as the real pivot point — vindicated, JT says, by the cascade of pipeline and refinery explosions and the Japan earthquake that followed. More broadly, Dan and Josh's viewers are at student level, working with a brand-new protocol they shouldn't be the first to test.

The wider polemic, joined by Morgan Farrell mid-Space, is generational and institutional. Astro RV could solve remote viewing's "time problem" — narrowing five-year prediction windows down to weeks — but only if it's executed by professionals, not turned into a circus for likes and clicks.

- JT calls out Dan Waites' World Astrology Report Astro RV sessions as "complete junk" — student-level viewers, wrong protocol choices, influencer-driven targeting

- Morgan joins around the midpoint, agrees fully, says he's "disappointed" — Astro RV "has been treated as a toy" for influencer status
- Broader generational critique: influencer-astrologers and influencer-RVers downplay training because they don't have it
- Defense of Ed Dames and Technical Remote Viewing (TRV) — Ed openly admitted he wasn't the best viewer, but he was the best at expanding and refining the discipline
