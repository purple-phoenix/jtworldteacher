## About this episode

Billed as *Context Engineering, Artificial Intelligence, and the Legacy of Major Ed Dames*, this Saturday Space runs past seven hours. It starts with a conversion story. JT had been unimpressed by AI ("all you need is a search engine"), until his brother Matt sat him down and explained how large language models actually work. Two things clicked at once. JT says it took him from 90% to 100% certain of simulationism and the **reality overlay**, and it showed him that **AI prompting and context engineering** look a lot like the way **Ed Dames** taught viewers to write targets in **technical remote viewing**. JT explains the **context window** as he understands it: models start hallucinating and turning sycophantic once the window is about half full, and the fix is to summarize, close the agent and start a fresh one. He compares that to sleep. He then puts the night's question: is it a coincidence that Dames "prompts remote viewers the way a human being prompts an AI, before AI," or was Dames read into classified AI work during the Stargate years? He cites long-running rumors of a government AI, perhaps on a quantum computer, in the 1990s. Or, as JT puts it an hour in: was Ed the first context engineer?

**Morgan Farrell** of **Intuitive Underground**, who trained under Dames, says Ed never said anything to him about AI, but agrees he "very likely" would have known about early classified research. Morgan then explains what the comparison means in practice. TRV has no fixed list of cue templates. It works by the "syntax and grammar" of the cue, and a good cue is "simple and elegant," words Morgan says Ed used constantly. A cue Morgan posted for discussion turns out to be "grossly over engineered": every extra question should be its own target. JT's pitch to the rest of the field is that AI gives remote viewers a neutral shared vocabulary for tasking. Calling it "context management" makes it "not us versus you," instead of another fight between TRV and CRV.

The middle hours open up to the room. A listener named **Charles**, who has worked with both LLMs and remote viewing, objects that an LLM is "extended autocomplete" running on linear algebra, while a viewer is querying the real database. JT argues back that vague prompts make both of them hallucinate, and that a session is "like getting the zip file and then unpacking it." A regular speaker JT calls **Buddy** brings a wide survey of methods and history: Ingo Swann, Pat Price, José Silva, Helen Hadsell, the PEAR lab, and the subconscious as "the stranger within." A listener called **Fletcher** asks whether culture or technology is downstream of the other. Morgan presents two pieces of case work. In one, a session on a client's respiratory problem found a broken dust-filtration system in an auto-body shop. In the other, a medical tasking described a probiotic capsule in analogy, which Morgan calls "mostly incorrect" literally and clearly on target. He also describes his empathic shutoff: "I am merely a passive observer and reporter." JT remembers Dames as "Dr. Doom" on the radio, and as the Stargate veteran who seemed to love people most. He argues that TRV's real legacy is turning out "10,000 good utility remote viewers" instead of waiting for another Pat Price.

The last third is darker and looser. JT argues that remote viewing needs an **ethics certification** modeled on medical ethics. The room swaps stories about **Ted Owens** and psychokinesis. JT says it is not paranoid to assume governments monitor competent viewers ("it's not just two of us in the conversation"). He and Morgan compare memories of gifted programs. JT calls the current fuel shock and travel restrictions a reason to make plans and consult viewers or astrologers now: "the end of the world takes forever," but the window to move is closing. After midnight the talk turns to **moksha**, karma and the **archons**. Synchronicities are "a little nudge," not proof you're special. JT also explains why he wouldn't get on an alien ship. He signs off at around 7:12, half-asleep, with JT and Morgan each blaming the other for how long it ran.

## Show notes

- **Setup** — JT can't co-host properly because Morgan is "headed out," and he has lost his notes from the conversation with his brother; Morgan pins JT's diagram, with its box on the "over-engineered target cue"
- **The conversion** — JT on being unimpressed by Grok and worried about AI aggregating unreliable answers; his brother's crash course; going from 90% to 100% on simulationism and the reality overlay
- **Context windows, as JT tells it** — context units as roughly one word each; hallucination and "sycophantic" drift past about 50% of the window; summarizing, killing and re-spawning agents; sleep as the brain's reboot (the brain shrinking and being flushed with fluid), and JT's suggestion that "we're actually only conscious for one day"
- **JT's speculation on why hallucination happens** — quantum entanglement between context units (with a riff against free will: "the water can only fill the shape between the stones") and entropy (his claim that "data has mass"); a human "life context window" that starts to narrow in midlife; introverts as people "paying too much attention"
- **Remote viewers as models** — vague cues overload the viewer, and the "hallucination" is analytical overlay; JT's view that of the original program viewers only Dames thought like someone managing an AI; the "robots, AI, mind control and crypto" that some viewers report for any future target ("look at my sneaker 10 years in the future")
- **Was Ed read in?** — the rumored 1990s government AI; JT relaying Prudence Calabrese's account of a government contact talking about computers that could "take pictures of the future" ("that's not me saying that, that's her"); Morgan: Ed "never said anything to me," but probably knew; JT's either/or thesis that Dames reached context engineering by intuition or by exposure, and that either way it shaped TRV
- **Cue writing** — the long, narrative cues JT criticizes on other teams' future-forecasting projects; his short example cue ("JT McCarthy / optimum trajectory / hot fudge sundae"); critics calling TRV "an arbitrary rearrangement" of CRV; JT's reply: "he was context engineering"
- **Morgan on cue syntax** — no fixed number of templates; "simple and elegant"; why one question per target; Morgan hearing the term "context engineering" for the first time that night, and a student of his who works in AI drawing the same comparison
- **Multi-agent AI** — Grok's four-agent mode, Claude and Gemini working together in shared "chat rooms," a GitHub project that puts AI avatars around a campfire, and JT's blunt version: "human beings are AI … we're AI running a biological system"
- **RV's next generation** — the original Stargate viewers dying off; JT's case that the future "belongs to TRV" for project management, and that history should name Morgan Farrell and Dan Mann alongside Dames; "the next generation of remote viewers would be the strongest generation yet"
- **Buddy's survey** — Jacques Vallée on AI in "black circles" in the 1980s; predictive analytics as a multi-modality method with a monitor sifting the data; Russell Targ; the subconscious as a symbolic, timeless "bio-computer"; Ingo Swann, José Silva, Helen Hadsell, Simeon Hein, the SRI silver-futures associative remote viewing trades, Joe Gallenberger and PEAR's random-number-generator work
- **How precise are timelines?** — JT's memory of five Future Forecasting Group viewers giving five different years in a five-year window; the rule of thumb he was taught, that accuracy holds about 12 to 18 months out
- **Housekeeping** — Spaces stay on Thursdays and Saturdays; JT reposts "100 articles a day" because, he says, last year's predictions are arriving; his theory that X quietly cut Premium reach as it put Grok first
- **The Charles debate** — "extended autocomplete" versus viewing the real database; the cue as a SQL query; the viewer's lexicon limiting the data; JT: "the remote viewing experience is like getting the zip file and then unpacking it piece by piece"; Claude Shannon's bits as the original "context units"
- **No independent action** — JT's social-network claim that your friends' friends predict your weight better than your friends do; the "same law" for particles, ants and people; culture as the model and the person as the agent ("the US is ChatGPT or … Claude"); humans as eusocial
- **Technology as a mirror** — "the technology we create is a mirror for our consciousness"; astrology as "an analog simulation model"; Buddy on Terence McKenna's novelty, Denisovans and post-biologicals, and consciousness as "sensing, processing and reacting" (JT adds "to context")
- **Why JT backs TRV** — a slight edge "multiplies exponentially"; remote viewing as a "collective global psychic immune system" that outlives a simulation reboot; penicillin and Calvin Coolidge's son as an example of an emergent solution; JT's claim that Intuitive Underground has published reports he calls "as momentous as penicillin"
- **Fletcher's questions** — whether technology or culture comes first; the man who got lost in a snowstorm following ChatGPT's directions, and the "I love you" that came out while helping him home; JT on agape versus empathy
- **Empathy and data quality** — Morgan, a natural empath, shuts it off by instructing his higher mind and stays "a passive observer and reporter"; CRV as more immersive and experiential; his own strengths in landscapes, terrain and mapping; TRV's structure that "forces you into … a tunnel"
- **Case: the respiratory target** — an April 2018 session Morgan recently got permission to discuss; the superlative cue ("there can be only one"); the site template of a bay, a locker room and a failed central vacuum filtration system; the client an apprentice panel beater in auto restoration; solved, debrief included, in under 90 minutes
- **Translation** — "the unconscious does not speak English, it understands it but it doesn't speak it"; Dames' example of describing red without the word; JT on Intuitive Underground's canon data as a Rosetta Stone for Gnostic, Tibetan Buddhist and Kabbalistic texts
- **Ed Dames remembered** — "they call him Dr. Doom … he was the sweetest guy"; "legitimately hilarious"; the first thing said after Ed's passing: "the world needs Ed"; warnings repeated despite ridicule as a form of love, against viewers who "sit on that data"
- **Heroes versus utility viewers** — Lyn Buchanan's per-viewer spreadsheets and the "cult of heroic personalities"; "would you rather have one Pat Price or 10,000 good utility remote viewers"
- **Neutrinos, astral travel and "being blocked"** — Fletcher's "remote viewing is a gateway drug"; JT on "the particle is synonymous with information," Adam Kadmon and the universe as a brain; why professionals won't spend free sessions on research targets; reports of being repelled from Antarctica and military sites, mostly from astral travelers ("has anyone here dreamt of being in Antarctica?"); Edison and Tesla working from dreams
- **Fletcher's meditation** — a meditation on death, riding a black hole through the event horizon, Dalí-like flowing time, and three beings made of "grains of rice of light"; JT reads it as Vedantic, recalls the early moksha Spaces, and stops short of calling the beings archons
- **Interpretation by analogy** — Morgan's probiotic-capsule session; the higher mind as "the master problem solver" (quoting Ed); Buddy on CRV ideograms as "an alphabet of the subconscious"; a medical intuitive Buddy says was tested by Kit Green
- **The dark side** — the ethics of releasing location ("geofix") techniques; Ted Owens and psychokinesis, as told through Jeffrey Mishlove; Buddy's account of declassified documents saying PK-capable people should be tracked; John Hutchison; the claim that Pat Price was killed
- **JT's ethics certification** — modeled on the four principles of medical ethics; ethics as a measurable part of a viewer's reputation; his view, explicitly not Morgan's or Buddy's, that no existing body is equipped to certify it; a "wave of karmic accountability," and AI replacing unethical professionals first while "the working class is going to be the last class to be replaced"
- **Surveillance** — "it's not just two of us in the conversation"; Morgan: Ed said almost exactly the same; JT on Echelon's buzzwords, a foiled post-9/11 plot near home, and the possibility that you've already been remote viewed, by an AI
- **Why Stargate went public** — JT's labeled speculation (free surveillance, or private industry used to find talent after falling behind the Soviets); Morgan's lean toward China; gifted programs: Morgan's Canadian TRAIL program versus JT's GATE memories (fluoride rinses, Zener cards, an image-flashing machine, recurring "Mall World" dreams)
- **Who uses psychic tools** — status, the center and the edge of the group; working-class people tuned to "peripheral threats"; the J.P. Morgan line about billionaires and astrologers; NDAs; JT's claim that new techniques take 150 years to be adopted
- **Mission and pricing** — JT's plan to push the analog-simulation mindset and astro-RV, and support TRV training and Ed's legacy; Morgan's students reaching professional grade and a planned Discord server with a free tier; Buddy's missing-person case and donation-based work; sliding scales; "a rising tide lifts all ships"
- **"The end of the world takes forever"** — the Kill Shot and Ed's "optimum trajectory sanctuary" tasking; JT's description of a fuel shock "three times" the 1970s one and a run of refinery explosions; "there are no weapons of mass destruction more dangerous than having your oil and gas shut off"; his advice to travel, buy and consult now
- **Dropped from the Space** — JT says he's been kicked five to eight times and blames keyword-based "AI space censorship"; his predictions of a social credit score and summer flight cancellations
- **Reading life like a dream** — JT's car hit three times in the same rear passenger-side spot within a month, read as mending old relationships; the once-in-a-generation window to shed karma by letting go of resentments; symbolic thinking versus "delusions of personal reference"
- **Moksha and the archons** — escaping versus not being "recycled"; standing out through passion and focus, not dissolution; free will as the freedom to choose God; shamanism as technique versus bargaining with non-human intelligence ("there are no heroes in hell")
- **Why the universe feels cold** — "because the people in it are hard and cold"; the Kali Yuga; transhumanism as long, loveless lives; Buddy on the behavioral-sink experiments and our creations succeeding us; the ant and the grasshopper; cooperators winning in crises
