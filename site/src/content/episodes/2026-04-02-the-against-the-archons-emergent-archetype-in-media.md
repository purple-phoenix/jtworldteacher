## About this episode

This Thursday Against the Archons Space examines how the Gnostic Archon problem is bubbling up through popular media as an emergent archetype. JT frames it through a Jungian lens: artists and writers bypass the social filter that censors unusual perceptions, so the collective unconscious leaks out first into taboo art forms like LitRPG, gaming fiction, and speculative TV. The same archetype has been emerging for roughly twenty years, accelerating until it is now manifesting as actual technology — Australian companies selling $35,000 computers that run on cultured human brain cells, taught first to play Pong and now Doom.

The Space is also a response to a Florida evangelical author who wrote a chapter accusing JT and Morgan Farrell of being deceived by demonic forces during their original Doom Room session on Intuitive Underground data. JT methodically dismantles this position by noting that for the accusation to hold, a non-human intelligence would have had to orchestrate thousands of years of Gnostic literature, decades of speculative fiction, the entire remote viewing record, and the live emergence of brain-based computing — all to deceive "a fat disabled guy in Massachusetts." That, he says, is the textbook definition of delusion of personal representation.

The throughline: the IU session data describes a reality overlay running on networked biological brains powered by the souls of political prisoners. Those exact details — neurological hardware, simulation inside a brain, captive consciousness as power source — are now the climactic reveals in Fallout, Dungeon Crawler Carl, Doctor Who and dungeon-core fiction broadly. The archetype is emerging on schedule.

## Show notes

- **Dungeon Crawler Carl** (Matt Dinniman, narrated by Jeff Hayes) — JT calls it the best audiobook series of all time. By Book 7, the reveal is that the planet is a nascent AI, every human has a recording node in their brain, and souls power the system when people die. Same architecture as the IU data.
- **Fallout** (Amazon TV series) — the Season 3 reveal is that the reality overlay is run from a mainframe that turns out to be a person's brain. JT cites the 30-year drift of the franchise toward this revelation as proof of emergent archetype.
- **Doctor Who** — flagged as another long-running science fiction property carrying the same core ideas at its center.
- **The Simpsons "predictions" phenomenon** — used as a defense of the methodology: when people say The Simpsons predicted events, they are unwittingly endorsing the theory of emergent archetypes appearing in cartoon form.
- **Zombie cinema (1970s-2010s)** — JT's earlier case study. He tried to analyze the archetype academically in 2011, was rebuffed by the taboo, then watched the COVID lockdowns deliver the archetype's full emergence
- **Dance Macabre paintings** — historical precedent. The peasants-dancing-with-skeletons motif emerged in art *before* the Black Death arrived, not as a response to it
- **Philip K. Dick / simulation theory** — JT pushes back on the Nick Bostrom origin story, noting Dick was asking "are we in a computer simulation" in the 1970s
- **Cortical Labs / biological computers** (Australia) — $35K commercial units running on cloned human brain tissue. Taught to play Pong in late 2024, Doom two weeks before this Space
- **9/11 imagery in pre-2001 media** — planes hitting the WTC appeared in heavy metal album covers, novels, and TV before the event
- **The unnamed evangelical anti-Gnostic book** — the trigger for the Space. JT argues its thesis requires reality itself to have been retrofitted across decades just to deceive him, Morgan, and Dan personally
